# Film Language Library

## Composition
Rule of thirds: align subject or leading edge with thirds when it helps hierarchy. Symmetry: use when stability/control is part of the meaning. Diagonal: use when force or instability should read. Negative space: reserve directional space in front of gaze/motion when continuity requires. Frames-within-frames: use architecture to isolate or reveal.

## Lighting
Three-point as a starting topology: key, fill, separation; modify by scene logic. Rembrandt: one-side key with controlled nose/cheek shadow. Butterfly: elevated frontal key. Hard light: crisp edge and high contrast. Soft light: broad transition. Practical sources must have a plausible origin unless intentionally stylized.

## Lens intent
Wide: spatial relationship, proximity, exaggeration. Normal: neutral relationship. Telephoto: compression, isolation, layered planes. Macro: material and micro-expression. The exact millimeter value is a technical choice after the visual intent is defined.

## Movement
Pan/tilt: reveal or track within fixed position. Dolly: spatial relationship changes. Tracking: accompany actor. Crane/jib: scale and geography. Handheld: human instability. Orbit: relational transformation; use sparingly because it can destroy spatial clarity if not motivated.

## Editing
Continuity cut preserves space/time. Match-on-action preserves movement. Graphic match links form. J-cut brings sound early. L-cut extends sound past picture. Parallel editing intercuts concurrent or contrasted actions. Montage creates meaning through juxtaposition.
